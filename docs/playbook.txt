Vernex Client Onboarding Playbook
Oct 12, 2026  ·  @BOSS
A new client goes from proposal to first approved work in about two weeks, through one
hard gate: a signed agreement and a paid first invoice. Everything after the gate runs from
the admin portal.
The flow at a glance
The top row is selling; the bottom row is onboarding. Nothing on the bottom row starts
until the gate is passed.
Step by step
Every new client gets the same 11 sends in the same order, from the proposal to the first
monthly report. Work starts only after two things are true: the agreement is signed and
the first invoice is paid. Skipping either is how agencies end up doing a month of work for
free.

Agreement checklist
Keep your existing 4-page format (3 pages of terms plus the signature page, on the
VerneX letterhead). Make sure every agreement covers the clauses below; the ones
marked new are not in the AGK Fitness agreement as described in the project notes. This
is a drafting checklist, not legal advice: have a lawyer review the master template once,
then reuse it.
Both service types:
Website projects only:
Parties, GSTINs, addresses, start date, signatories
Scope: deliverables per month or per project, listed specifically
Out of scope, and how extra work is quoted (change request) new
Fees, GST on top, payment due dates, late-payment terms and the right to pause work
when an invoice is 14+ days overdue new
Ad spend paid by the client directly to Meta or Google; 50% ad-budget advance before
campaigns launch
Third-party costs (domain, hosting, plugins, stock assets, booking engines) paid by the
client
Revision limits per deliverable, and that extra rounds are paid new
Approval rule: feedback within 3 working days, or the item counts as approved;
approvals given in the portal are binding new
Client responsibilities: access, brand assets, timely feedback, a single decision-maker
KPIs with baseline, target and measurement period; results are indicative, not
guaranteed
Ownership: the client owns paid-for final work; Vernex keeps working files and may
show the work in its portfolio unless the client opts out new
Confidentiality, and handling of the client's customer data under India's DPDP Act new
Term, renewal and termination (for cause or non-payment during the fixed term)
Account handover on exit: Vernex removes its own access and returns all assets new
Governing law: India, courts at Chennai
Payment in milestones: 50% advance, 30% on design approval, 20% before launch
Number of design rounds included, and the page list
Content: who writes copy and supplies photos, and by when; delays on the client side
move the launch date

Invoices and payments
Bill retainers in advance, on the 1st of each month, due within 7 days; bill websites in
milestones. Never start a month's work on an unpaid invoice. Confirm the tax points
below with your CA once; they depend on Vernex's GST registration.
Fields every invoice needs (GST-registered): Vernex legal name, address and GSTIN;
invoice number in one series (INV-0001, INV-0002…); invoice date; client name, address
and GSTIN; place of supply (state); description of the service and the month; SAC code;
taxable value; GST rate and amount; total in figures and words; bank and UPI details;
signature.
Tax quick reference (check with your CA):
Digital marketing and web design are charged GST at 18%.
Client in Tamil Nadu: CGST 9% + SGST 9%. Client in another state: IGST 18%.
Client outside India: usually an export of services, zero-rated under a Letter of
Undertaking (LUT), billed in their currency.
If Vernex is not yet GST-registered (services turnover under ₹ 20 lakh a year), do not
add GST; issue a plain invoice.
Business clients may deduct TDS before paying. Ask at onboarding so the shortfall isn't
a surprise.
Payment schedule:
Source files and credentials handed over only after final payment
Warranty: 30 days of free bug fixes after launch; changes after that are paid
Optional maintenance plan as a separate monthly fee

Discovery questionnaire (send as a form):
1. What should a visitor do on the site: call, WhatsApp, book, buy or enquire?
2. Who is the ideal customer, and what do they search for?
3. Services and prices to show, and which matters most?
4. Three websites you like, and why?
5. Must-have features: booking, payments, blog, multilingual (Tamil, Hindi), gallery?
6. Who writes the copy, and who approves it?
7. Launch deadline, and anything tied to it (an event or an offer)?
Launch checklist: mobile check on 2 real phones, page speed checked, forms tested end
to end, SSL on, 301 redirects from old pages, GA4 and pixel firing, Google Business Profile
link updated, backup taken, credentials and a short how-to video handed over.
After launch: offer a maintenance plan (updates, backups, small edits) as a monthly fee,
and suggest the next step: ads or SEO to bring traffic to the new site.
